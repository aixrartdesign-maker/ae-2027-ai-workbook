[3758] What Is AI? — History and Today's Generative AI <span class="lv">●○○</span>
[3759] AI did not suddenly appear in 2022. More than 70 years of research have reached the point where you can “just say it and get images and video.” This chapter sums up in one place what AI is, how it got here, and which AIs have already entered AE work today.
[3760] 1-1 What Is AI? — From Rules to Learning
[3761] <b>Artificial intelligence (AI)</b> is the whole family of technologies that let computers do work that people used to do with their intelligence. At first, people wrote the rules in one by one—“lots of red pixels and round means apple.” But real apples are so varied that the rules never ended.
[3762] So the approach changed. Instead of writing rules, you <b>show the computer a huge number of examples</b> and let it find what they have in common—this is <b>machine learning</b>. Of these methods, the one that uses many stacked layers of computation modeled on the brain's neural networks is called <b>deep learning</b>, and almost all the AI we use today is deep learning.
[3763] Model
[3764] The trained AI itself. “Seedance 2.0”, “Claude” and “Firefly Image 5” are all model names
[3765] Training data
[3766] The text, images and video a model learned from. What it learned shapes the quality of results and copyright issues
[3767] Prompt
[3768] The instruction you give a model. Every PROMPT box in this book is a prompt
[3769] Generative AI
[3770] AI that goes beyond classifying and judging to create new text, images, sound and video
[3771] Agent
[3772] AI that doesn't just answer but uses tools directly to finish the job. Claude operating AE through the bridge is an example of an agent
[3773] The usage unit deducted each time you generate. It varies by model, resolution and length (4-2, 3-8)

[3792] 1-3 How Does Generative AI Make Things?
[3793] Each kind of generative AI makes things in a different way. Knowing the rough principle helps you understand “why fingers come out strange” and “why the same prompt gives different results.”
[3794] Principle (very briefly)
[3795] Use in AE work · Main models
[3796] Text (LLM)
[3797] Looks at the words so far and picks the next word one at a time by probability
[3798] Planning, subtitles, writing expressions, operating AE · Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini
[3799] Image
[3800] Starts from noise and gradually removes it to match the prompt (diffusion)
[3801] Backgrounds, props, style frames · Firefly, GPT Image, Nano Banana, FLUX
[3802] Video
[3803] Extends image generation along the time axis, making frames together so they connect
[3804] B-roll, changing scenes, bringing images to life · Seedance, Kling, Veo, Runway
[3805] Sound
[3806] Generates the waveform of voices and music, or their features
[3807] Narration, dubbing, sound effects · ElevenLabs, etc.
[3808] Segmentation & tracking
[3809] Finds the edges of objects on screen and follows them frame by frame
[3810] Background removal, rotoscoping · AE Object Matte, Roto Brush
[3811] <b>Why results differ every time</b>Generative AI starts from different noise and probabilities every time. So the same prompt gives different results, and to get one you like you either generate several times (credits) or narrow things down with reference images and a seed. AE keyframes, by contrast, render identically no matter how many times you render. That is why this book's basic flow is “make it fast with AI, fix it precisely in AE.”

[3823] <span class="tag t1">Follow along</span> 2-3 Installing AE and Updating to the Latest Version

[3867] <span class="tag t1">Follow along</span> 5-2 Connecting Higgsfield to ChatGPT
[3868] Log in to ChatGPT (web or desktop app). You need a paid Higgsfield subscription to generate (4-2).

[3872] <span class="lbl">PROMPT · to ChatGPT</span>Use Higgsfield to make one 16:9 image with the cheapest model: an alley at night in the rain with neon signs, no people. Before generating, tell me how many credits it will cost.

[3875] 5-3 Adding Other MCP Servers to ChatGPT — When You Want the Bridge
[3876] ChatGPT can add remote MCP servers directly. Use this for servers not in the plugin list, such as the Higgsfield bridge (<span class="mono">bridge.higgsfield.ai/mcp</span>).
[3877] On ChatGPT's Plugins screen, choose <b>Add</b> → <b>Create custom MCP server</b>. On older screens you first had to turn on <b>Settings → Apps → Advanced settings → Developer mode</b>.
[3878] Enter a name (e.g., Higgsfield Bridge) and the server address, then create it.
[3879] When the login window opens, log in to Higgsfield yourself.
[3880] With the Higgsfield panel open in AE and Bridge MCP's <b>Connect</b> pressed, test it by typing “Show me the list of compositions in the AE project that's open now” in the chat.
[3881] <b>Plans and permissions</b>Which plans can use custom MCP servers, and how far write (control) permissions go, have varied by ChatGPT plan and over time. This book verified bridge control only in Claude, so in other AIs first test with a short task to see whether you get the same result.

[3906] What Is a Character? — Design, Rigging, Keeping the Same Character with AI <span class="lv">●○○ ~ ●●○</span>
[3907] Before moving the robot joint by joint in the next chapter, let's look at what a “character” is and how to prepare one. Whether you animate by hand or make it with AI, good characters meet the same conditions.
[3908] Success means choosing your own character and making a simple two-view character sheet (front and side).
[3909] 14-1 What Is a Character?
[3910] A <b>character</b> is a being in a video that thinks, moves and drives the story. It doesn't have to be a person. Animals, robots, school mascots, talking logos, even a milk carton with eyes and a mouth are characters if they have a personality and behave.
[3911] Characters in motion graphics
[3912] Brand mascot
[3913] Guides in company or school promo videos, social media stickers
[3914] Explainer video host
[3915] A figure who explains how to use an app or a public campaign
[3916] Personified objects
[3917] Data videos where chart bars grow while smiling
[3918] AI characters
[3919] Virtual models and influencers made with AI. The challenge is keeping the same face in every scene (14-6)
[3920] 14-2 What Makes a Good Character
[3921] Condition
[3922] How to check
[3923] Silhouette
[3924] Can you tell who it is even when filled in black? Hairstyle, ears and props create the silhouette
[3925] Proportions
[3926] Head-to-body ratio (2–3 heads tall is cute, 6–8 is realistic). Are the joints to animate clear?
[3927] 2–3 main colors. Does it stand out against backgrounds?
[3928] Personality
[3929] Does the standing pose and expression alone show its personality? Once the personality is set, walking speed and gestures follow
[3930] Simplicity
[3931] The fewer lines and parts, the easier it is to animate—and the better AI keeps it looking the same
[3932] <span class="tag t1">Follow along</span> 14-3 Making a Character Sheet
[3933] A <b>character sheet</b> is a blueprint showing one character from several angles and with several expressions. It keeps the character looking the same even when animators change or when you ask AI for many scenes.
[3934] On paper or in Illustrator, draw the full body from the <b>front</b>. Line the height up with a guide line.
[3935] At the same height, draw the <b>side</b> view (and 3/4 and back views if needed). This set of views is called a <b>turnaround</b>.
[3936] Below, draw 4–6 <b>expressions</b> (neutral, smiling, surprised, angry, sad), faces only.
[3937] Next to it, note the <b>color swatches</b> (HEX values) and height and proportion notes. In AE, put these colors in a control layer (18-16).
[3938] Save the finished sheet as a PNG in the project's <span class="mono">99_Guide</span> folder. You'll also use it as an AI reference image in 14-6.
[3939] 14-4 Three Ways to Move a Character in AE
[3940] How
[3941] Best for
[3942] Part layers + parenting
[3943] Split head, torso, arms and legs into layers and parent them at each joint (15-3, 15-4)
[3944] Robots, paper dolls, flat illustrations
[3945] Stick pins in a single drawing and bend it like rubber (16-6)
[3946] Tails, hair, wobbly mascots
[3947] Rigging plugins
[3948] Build limb IK and facial controls with tools such as Duik and RubberHose (Appendix C)
[3949] Full character animation with lots of dialogue and walking
[3950] <span class="tag t1">Follow along</span> 14-5 Preparing a Character in Illustrator or Photoshop
[3951] Split the drawing into <b>a separate layer for each moving part</b>: head, torso, upper arm, forearm, hand, thigh, shin, foot (left and right).
[3952] So no gaps show when joints bend, draw the ends of connecting parts <b>rounded and overlapping</b> (circles at elbows and knees).
[3953] Give the layers short English names (<span class="mono">Head</span>, <span class="mono">Arm_Upper_L</span>…). You'll use these exact names in expressions and AI instructions.
[3954] When importing into AE with <b>File → Import → File</b>, set Import As to <b>Composition - Retain Layer Sizes</b> (25-5). Each part comes in as its own layer.
[3955] Move each part's Anchor Point to the <b>joint</b>. With the <b>Pan Behind Tool</b> (<span class="k">Y</span>) in the toolbar, drag the upper arm's to the shoulder and the forearm's to the elbow. You're now ready for the parenting in the next chapter.
[3956] 14-6 AI Characters — Keeping the Same Person in Every Scene
[3957] The hardest part of making character videos with AI is <b>consistency</b>, because the face, clothes and proportions drift a little from scene to scene. Layering the following methods greatly reduces this.
[3958] Reference images
[3959] Include the 14-3 character sheet every time. Giving front and side together keeps turning shots from falling apart
[3960] Fixed description
[3961] Decide in one paragraph the features that never change—like “yellow raincoat, round glasses, a robot 2.5 heads tall”—and use the same sentences every time
[3962] Saved character features
[3963] Save the person with features such as Higgsfield's characters (person reference) or Kling's Elements, and call them by name
[3964] Seed · same model
[3965] Record the seed and model of a result you like and don't change them
[3966] Finishing in AE
[3967] Add logos, costume colors and subtitles in AE on top of the AI-made motion, and brand elements stay exactly the same in every scene
[3968] <b>When using a real person as a character</b>To make an AI character from a real person's face or voice, you need that person's consent. Don't use photos of celebrities or other people as references, and check usage rights for school and company mascots too (Ch. 29).
[3969] <b>If it works</b>A character sheet is in the 99_Guide folder, and the character file, split into parts, is in AE as layers.
[3970] <b>If you're stuck</b>If the parts come in merged into one image, Import As is set to Footage. Import again with Composition - Retain Layer Sizes.
