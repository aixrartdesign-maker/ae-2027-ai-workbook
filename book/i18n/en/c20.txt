[2790] Ch. 17 AI Assistant
[2791] Neither connection needs an API key. Add the address in Claude’s connector settings and log in with your Higgsfield account (18-5). The result is an ordinary .aep file, so the biggest advantage is that a person can always fix the layers AI made by hand.
[2792] In Claude, go to <b>Customize → Connectors → + Add → Add custom connector</b>, enter the address, and log in to Higgsfield in the window that opens (18-5). You never need to give Claude your password.
[2793] <b>Another use of Replace Footage</b>It swaps only the original and keeps effects, keyframes and masks. Use the same method to swap a 480p practice clip for the upscaled final (19-5). Deleted files can’t be relinked, so restore them from the Recycle Bin or swap in a file with the same content.
[2794] <b>Check before rendering</b>An eye left off, Solo left on or fx left off changes the render. Make a habit of scanning the switch columns right before you render. You can also ask the AI Assistant from Chapter 17 to “find disabled layers and solo states.”
[2795] <b>Compared with Higgsfield</b>Change Weather in 20-1 wets the ground and changes the light in one go. CC Rainfall only makes streaks, but you can adjust thickness, amount and direction frame by frame, and it costs no credits. Layering both—adding only foreground streaks on top of the AI result—also works well.
[2796] <b>The bridge’s limit</b>As seen in Chapter 22, the current Claude bridge could not move Range Selector keyframes. A realistic split is to let AI build the animator’s skeleton and adjust the timing by hand.
[2797] <b>Compared with AI</b>Higgsfield Remove BG (Ch. 19) extracts people without green cloth, and AE’s Object Matte (Ch. 11) also makes a matte with one click. Broadcast and film still use chroma key because it can extract even semi-transparent parts — a single hair, a glass, smoke — precisely. As of 2026, AI mattes are fast but still fall short of chroma key in edge quality and frame-to-frame consistency.
[2798] Higgsfield’s <b>Motion Control</b> (20-4) is an application of the AI markerless method. It extracts the motion from a reference video and applies it to another person’s image. In AE, the character video made this way is cut out with Keylight or Remove BG and composited with a background.
[2799] <b>Doing it with AI</b>Higgsfield Change Time of Day (20-2) changes not only the sky but also the light on the buildings. By hand only the sky changes and you match the light yourself, but you keep full control of the original quality and the result.
[2800] <b>Compared with Higgsfield Draw to Edit</b>Content-Aware Fill keeps the original quality and is free, but leaves traces on complex backgrounds. Draw to Edit (19-4) repaints for a natural look, but costs credits and may change the resolution. Use AE for simple objects and AI generation for complex scenes.
[2801] <b>Substance materials (.sbsar)</b>: import materials from Adobe Substance 3D Assets and choose them from the Material dropdown. The maps are inside the material, and you change color and roughness with sliders (17-7).
[2802] <b>Layers as materials (27.0)</b>: use a composition, video or image as Base Color. Good for moving screens and signs (17-7).
[2803] As Composition - Retain Layer Sizes (14-5). To edit Illustrator text, use SVG (17-7)
[2804] Run .jsx files with <b>File → Scripts → Run Script File</b>, and put scripts you use often in the Scripts folder to use them as panels from the Window menu. For repetitive work such as batch renaming and arranging layers. Today the AI Assistant (Chapter 17) and the Claude bridge (Chapter 22) take over much of this role

[2819] Inside the Assistant you can create backgrounds, reference images and short videos with Firefly and partner models and put them straight into the project. Generation uses many credits, so check the rates in 17-8 first.
[2820] <b>Which generation, where?</b>For sources you'll use right away inside After Effects—a background, a texture—the Assistant is convenient. For comparing many models or keeping the same character across shots, Higgsfield (Chapters 19–21) is better. Either way, check the commercial-use terms (17-8) and make sure no real people or trademarks appear.
