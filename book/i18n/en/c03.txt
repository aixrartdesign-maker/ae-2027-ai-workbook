[493] <span class="tag t1">Follow Along</span> 4-3 Moving a Robot Body — Parenting at the Joints
[494] Like a paper doll, split the body into layers by segment, move each segment’s <b>Anchor Point</b> to its joint, and link it to <b>the segment closer to the body as parent</b>. Then rotating just the shoulder moves the whole arm, and moving just the waist moves the whole upper body. A skeleton built this way is called a <b>rig</b>. The white robot in the practice file <span class="mono">04-3_로봇리그</span> was cut from one <b>parts sheet</b> drawn with Higgsfield GPT Image.
[495] <b>Three rules</b>① <b>Split</b> — one layer is one segment between two joints. If the upper arm and forearm are on one image, the elbow cannot bend. ② <b>Anchor point</b> — put it at the segment’s <b>joint nearest the body</b>: shoulder for the upper arm, elbow for the forearm, wrist for the hand. ③ <b>Parent</b> — the segment nearer the body is the parent. Link hand → forearm → upper arm → torso → pelvis → Master_Control (null).

[532] <b>Making the parts sheet.</b> In Higgsfield, first make one piece of character art (front and side), then add it as a reference and request a <b>parts sheet</b> as below. Remove the background with Remove Background and download a PNG. If you draw it yourself, save each part on its own layer in Illustrator or Photoshop.
[533] <span class="lbl">PROMPT · Higgsfield GPT Image (1 reference image)</span>Character rig parts sheet of this exact robot (FRONT VIEW), same colors and style. Lay out every body part separated with generous empty space, all upright, on a plain pure white background: head, torso with shoulders, pelvis, upper arms, forearms, open five-finger hands, thighs, shins, boots. Each limb part has a full round gray ball joint at its TOP end so parts can overlap and rotate seamlessly. No part touches another. No text, no shadows.
[534] <b>Cut one image into as many parts as needed.</b> Place the same PNG in the composition once per part and use a rectangle mask on each layer to keep only one part. Name each layer with the part and side, such as <span class="mono">UpperArm_L</span>. The practice file has 15 parts plus 1 null, 16 layers in all.
[535] <b>Anchor points to the joints.</b> With the <b>Pan Behind (Anchor Point) tool</b> (<span class="k">Y</span>), drag the anchor point to the center of the ball joint. This tool moves only the anchor point and leaves the image where it is. Then move Position to assemble each part onto the body.
[536] <b>Parenting and stacking order.</b> Drag the pick whip from the end toward the body (hand → forearm → upper arm → torso → pelvis → Master_Control). Parenting and stacking order are separate. On this robot the ball joint is at the <b>top end</b> of each segment, so stack <b>the lower segment on top</b> so the ball covers the end of the segment above (the hand above the forearm, not the forearm above the hand). The upper arms and thighs, however, go <b>under</b> the torso and pelvis so the shoulder caps and pelvis shell hide the tops of the joints.
[537] <b>Animate Rotation only.</b> Now each segment needs only Rotation. Rotate the segment nearer the body first and let the end segment follow 2–4 frames later for a natural, whip-like motion. This is called <b>overlap (follow-through)</b>. In 4-4 we use this rig to make a walk.

[549] <b>Animator’s check — hiding the seams</b>① Draw joints as <b>round balls (ball joints)</b>. If the ball’s center is the anchor point, the two parts overlap with no gap however far they bend. ② Make the ball joints <b>a different color (gray)</b> from the body, and even with shading the overlap reads naturally as a “joint between parts”. ③ Stacking shells that wrap the joints, such as shoulder caps and the pelvis, on top hides the upper part of the ball so it looks like a real joint. ④ Draw parts in a straight standing pose from the start so rotation 0 is the default pose. ⑤ Characters with simple shapes — robots, animals, mascots — suit cut-out rigs better than humans.
[550] <b>If you are stuck</b>It rotates around the wrong point → the anchor point is in the middle. Move it to the ball joint’s center with the <span class="k">Y</span> tool. A part jumps away when you parent it → if you hold <span class="k">Shift</span> while using the pick whip, the child moves to the parent’s position; a plain drag leaves it in place. The child changes size too → you changed the parent’s Scale. Change size only on Master_Control. A gap opens at a joint → the anchor point is off the ball’s center, or the stacking order has changed.
[551] <b>Going further</b>For characters with many joints, the free script <b>Duik</b> builds IK (drag the fingertip and the whole arm bends to follow). To bend limbs like rubber, put Puppet pins (5-6) on a single-piece arm.
[552] <span class="tag t1">Follow Along</span> 4-4 A Side-View Walk — One Cycle from Four Poses
[553] A walk is <b>four poses repeated</b>. The foot touches the ground (contact), takes the weight and sinks (down), the other foot passes the body (passing), and it pushes off the toes and rises (up). At 30 fps one step is 15 frames, and two steps — 30 frames (1 second) — make one cycle. Keyframe only the four poses, AE fills in between, and with <span class="mono">loopOut()</span> at the end it keeps walking. Composition <span class="mono">04-4_로봇걷기</span>.
[554] <b>Figure 4-5</b>Parts sheet for the side-view walk (facing left). Each limb segment has a ball joint at its top end, and the red dot is the anchor point. Arm and leg parts are drawn once and imported twice for the front and back.
[555] <b>Figure 4-6</b>The four poses in the practice file. The robot faces left. The light limbs are the near (front) side and the dark limbs are the far (back) side. From frame 15 the front and back legs swap and the same four poses repeat.
[556] <b>How the side-view rig differs</b>① Make <b>two sets</b> of limbs, <b>front (near side) and back (far side)</b>, and add _Front and _Back to the names. Just import the same parts twice. ② Darken the back set with an Exposure effect of about −0.7 and move those layers to the bottom. Stacking order from the top: front arm (hand, forearm, upper arm) → head → torso → pelvis → front leg (foot, shin, thigh) → back leg → back arm. ③ Within one arm or leg, stack the lower segment on top so the ball joint covers the end of the segment above. ④ For a character facing left, <b>+</b> Rotation sends a leg forward (left) and <b>−</b> sends it back. The shin only ever bends to the − side (so the knee never bends backwards).
