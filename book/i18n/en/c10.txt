[1247] <span class="tag t1">Follow Along</span> 13-8 Adjusting Materials and Textures
[1248] Flat layers and 3D models handle materials in different places.
[1249] <b>Flat 3D layers</b>: expand the layer to <b>Material Options</b>. Change Diffuse (brightness under light), Specular Intensity (strength of shine), Specular Shininess (how tight and sharp the shine is) and Metal (how much the layer color mixes into reflections).
[1250] <b>3D models, 3D text and shapes</b>: select the layer in Advanced 3D and <b>Material Assignment</b> appears in the Properties panel and timeline. The default material properties are Base Color, Roughness, Metallic, Emission Color and Intensity, and Ambient Response. PBR values from the model file come in as they are.
[1251] <b>Substance 3D materials (.sbsar)</b>: from 26.0 they can be applied to models and parametric meshes, and from 27.0 to 3D text and shapes too. In 27.0 you can also use a video or composition layer as the source of a material.
[1252] To change the texture image itself, edit it in Blender or Substance, export the GLB again, and swap it with right-click → <b>Replace Footage → File</b> (<span class="k">Ctrl+H</span>) in the Project panel.

[1265] <span class="tag t1">Follow Along</span> 13-9 Orbiting the Camera on a Null
[1266] Keyframing a camera directly makes circular moves hard. Instead, put a null at the center of the subject and make the camera its child, and <b>just rotating the null makes the camera circle</b>. It is the same principle as rotating a shoulder to make the hand draw a circle (4-3). Composition <span class="mono">13-4_카메라널궤도</span>.
[1267] Animated property
[1268] Eye
[1269] Orbit_Null
[1270] Usually fixed. Position <span class="mono">0, 0, −2667</span>, Point of Interest <span class="mono">0, 0, 0</span>
[1271] Orbit_Null (3D)
[1272] Rotation axis
[1273] Crane_Null
[1274] Y Rotation = turning left and right, X Rotation = looking over from above
[1275] Crane_Null (3D)
[1276] Height and position
[1277] Position Y = crane up, Position X = sideways move (truck)
[1278] Make <b>Layer → New → Null Object</b> twice and rename them <span class="mono">Orbit_Null</span> and <span class="mono">Crane_Null</span>. Turn on the <b>3D switch</b> (cube column) for both. If it is off, you only see one Rotation and no Y Rotation.
[1279] Put both nulls’ Position at the center you want to orbit, usually the middle of the subject (e.g. <span class="mono">960, 540, 0</span>). Parent Orbit_Null to Crane_Null.
[1280] Parent the camera to Orbit_Null. Then select the camera and set <span class="k">A</span> (Point of Interest) to <span class="mono">0, 0, 0</span> and <span class="k">P</span> (Position) to <span class="mono">0, 0, −2667</span>. A child’s values are relative to its parent, so the camera looks at the null from 2667 px in front of it. 2667 is this camera’s Zoom value; at this distance, layers at z=0 appear at their original size.
[1281] Select Orbit_Null, press <span class="k">R</span>, and keyframe <b>Y Rotation</b> (0° at 0 s, 0x+360° at 4 s), or add the expression below.
[1282] Decrease Crane_Null’s Position Y (upward) and the camera rises and looks down. Because orbiting and rising are on separate nulls, you can adjust them independently.
[1283] <b>Figure 13-9</b>The timeline of the practice file. ① Orbit_Null’s parent is 2 Crane_Null, with an expression on Y Rotation ② an up-and-down expression on Crane_Null Position ③ Camera 1’s parent is 1 Orbit_Null.

[1285] <span class="lbl">EXPRESSION · Moving the Camera with Nulls</span>// Orbit_Null Y Rotation — one turn in 4 s<br/>time * 90;<br/>// Orbit_Null Y Rotation — swing 35° left and right (practice file)<br/>Math.sin(time*Math.PI/2) * 35;<br/>// Crane_Null Position — rise 300 px and come down over 2 s<br/>value + [0, -Math.sin(time*Math.PI/4)*300, 0];

[1302] <b>If you are stuck</b>The picture changes a lot when you parent → the camera Position is now read relative to the parent. Enter <span class="mono">0, 0, −distance</span> again as in step 3. The camera looks somewhere odd → check that Point of Interest is <span class="mono">0, 0, 0</span>. After a full turn you see the edge of the background or the back of a card → the background is a single flat plane. Keep it to a left-right swing as in the practice file, or fill all sides with 3D models and an Environment Light.
[1303] <b>If it worked</b>When you move the camera and lights in 4 Views, the perspective and shadows in the Active Camera view change with them.
[1304] <b>If you are stuck</b>If you can’t see shadows, check three places: the light’s Casts Shadows, Material Options → Casts Shadows on the layer casting the shadow, and Accepts Shadows on the layer receiving it. Ambient lights alone make no shadows.
[1305] Render the same GLB model twice, with Roughness 0.1 and 0.8, and compare them side by side. Then switch the Environment Light HDR from indoor to outdoor and note how the scenery reflected in the metal surface changes.
