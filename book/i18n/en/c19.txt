[2480] <b>Orientation versus X, Y, Z Rotation</b>: a 3D layer has two sets of rotation values. <b>Orientation</b> sets “which way it faces”; when keyframed it always takes the shortest path, so it can't turn more than 360°. <b>X, Y, Z Rotation</b> can be keyframed per axis and allows multiple turns, like “2x+30°.” Use Orientation for the starting angle and Rotation for motion, and nothing gets tangled.

[2493] 13-11 Understanding Texture Maps — The Seven Images of a PBR Material
[2494] A 3D model's surface is defined not by a few numbers but by <b>several images (maps)</b>. Today's 3D software, After Effects' Advanced 3D and Substance all use <b>PBR</b> (physically based rendering, Metal-Roughness workflow). Knowing the map names alone lets you understand downloaded models and materials.

[2496] Looks like
[2497] Color photo
[2498] The surface's own color. It must not contain shadows or highlights
[2499] Black and white
[2500] Black is smooth (mirror), white is rough (matte). Fingerprints and scratches go here
[2501] Black and white (usually 0 or 1)
[2502] Only white areas reflect like metal. On rusty iron, only the rust is black
[2503] Purple-blue image

[2506] Color, mostly black

[2509] <b>Three ways to use maps in After Effects</b>
[2510] <b>Pack maps into the model file</b>: apply maps in Blender or Substance Painter and export as <b>GLB</b>; the maps travel inside the file and appear as soon as you import it into After Effects. The most reliable way.
[2511] <b>Substance materials (.sbsar)</b>: import materials from Adobe Substance 3D Assets and choose them from the Material dropdown. The maps are inside the material, and you change color and roughness with sliders (23-7).
[2512] <b>Layers as materials (27.0)</b>: use a composition, video or image as Base Color. Good for moving screens and signs (23-7).
[2513] <b>Follow Along — making metal look like metal with an HDRI</b>
[2514] Place a metal model (or extruded 3D text with Metallic 1, Roughness 0.2) in an Advanced 3D composition. At first it looks like a gray lump. Metal is an object that <b>reflects its surroundings</b>, so with nothing to reflect it looks dull.
[2515] Import an HDRI file (.hdr or .exr, 2K–4K) and place it at the bottom of the composition. You can get free HDRIs from sites such as Poly Haven.
[2516] Choose <b>Layer → New → Light</b>, set Light Type to <b>Environment</b>, and click OK. Set <b>Source</b> in Light Options to the HDRI layer you just placed.
[2517] Turn off the HDRI layer's eye. It isn't seen as a background; it is used only for reflections and light.
[2518] Adjust the environment light's Intensity (start at 100%) and Y Rotation to find an angle where the reflection sweeps across the text. Keyframe that angle and you get a premium logo with flowing light.
[2519] 13-12 Element 3D — The Best-Known 3D Plug-in
[2520] Video Copilot's <b>Element 3D</b> is a paid plug-in that draws 3D models quickly on the GPU inside After Effects. Since before Advanced 3D existed it has been a standard for 3D logos, type and spaceship scenes, and many market templates still require it. Its strengths are <b>OBJ and C4D model import</b>, text <b>extrusion</b>, rich material presets, the <b>Particle Replicator</b> that copies one model hundreds of times, and integration with After Effects cameras and lights.
[2521] Create a black solid (<span class="k">Ctrl+Y</span>) and apply <b>Effect → Video Copilot → Element</b>. If the composition has no camera, first create one with <b>Layer → New → Camera</b>. Element shoots the scene through the After Effects camera.
[2522] To extrude text, create a text layer, turn off its eye, and choose that text layer under Element's <b>Custom Layers → Custom Text and Masks → Path Layer 1</b>.
[2523] Click <b>Scene Setup</b> at the top of Effect Controls to open Element's own window. Click the <b>EXTRUDE</b> button at the top and the text rises in 3D. To use a model, load an OBJ file with <b>IMPORT</b> or choose one from the <b>Model Browser</b> on the left.
[2524] Drag a material (metal, glass, chrome, etc.) from <b>Presets</b> at the bottom onto the model. Change the Diffuse, Specularity, Reflectivity, Normal Bump and Illumination textures in the <b>Edit</b> panel on the right. The maps from 13-11 are used here directly.
[2525] Click <b>OK</b> to return to After Effects. Change Particle Count and Replicator Shape (Plane, Ring, Sphere, etc.) under <b>Group 1 → Particle Replicator</b> in Effect Controls and the model multiplies into an array.
[2526] Turn on Ambient Occlusion and Motion Blur in <b>Render Settings</b>, and set <b>Lighting</b> to use After Effects lights. If it's heavy, keep Render Mode at Preview while working.

[2544] <span class="tag t1">Follow Along</span> 14-8 One Lap Around the Render Queue — Reading Every Setting
[2545] Select the composition to render in the Project panel and choose <b>Composition → Add to Render Queue</b> (<span class="k">Ctrl+M</span>). The Render Queue panel opens at the bottom with one row.
[2546] Click the blue text next to <b>Render Settings</b> (Best Settings by default). Check that Quality is <b>Best</b> and Resolution is <b>Full</b>, then look at <b>Time Span</b> below. <b>Work Area Only</b> renders just the work area; <b>Length of Comp</b> renders everything. This is the most common reason for a cut-off video.
[2547] Click the blue text next to <b>Output Module</b> (H.264 or High Quality by default). Set the items in the table below and click OK.
[2548] Click the blue text next to <b>Output To</b> to choose the folder and file name. Use the project folder's <span class="mono">render</span> folder.
[2549] Click <b>Render</b> at the top right. When it finishes you hear a sound and the row turns green. To output the same composition in several formats, click <b>+</b> to the left of the Output Module row to add another output (MP4 and ProRes at once).
[2550] Output Module item
[2551] How to choose
[2552] H.264 (MP4), QuickTime (MOV), AVI, PNG Sequence, JPEG Sequence, TIFF Sequence, OpenEXR Sequence, WAV, etc.
[2553] Codec and quality. For QuickTime, Video Codec (ProRes 422 HQ, ProRes 4444, etc.); for H.264, bitrate; for JPEG, Quality
[2554] <b>RGB</b> (usual) / <b>RGB + Alpha</b> (transparent background) / Alpha (black-and-white matte only)
[2555] Millions of Colors / <b>Millions of Colors+</b> (automatic with alpha). High-end deliveries use Trillions (16-bit)
[2556] Premultiplied (Matted, default) / Straight (Unmatted). Follow the recipient's spec
[2557] Resize to deliver a 1080p version of something made in 4K at the same time
[2558] Choose <b>Import &amp; Replace Usage</b> and the render comes into the project and replaces the original composition where it's used (pre-render)
[2559] Auto / On / Off, 48 kHz. If there's no sound, check whether this is Off

[2583] <span class="tag t1">Follow Along</span> 14-10 Rendering with a Transparent Background (Alpha)
[2584] To lay caption or logo animations over Premiere or other video, you need a file with a transparent background. In After Effects, <b>wherever there is nothing</b> is transparent.
[2585] Turn off or delete the background solid in the composition. The Background Color in Composition Settings isn't rendered, so you don't need to worry about it.
[2586] Click <b>Toggle Transparency Grid</b> (the checkerboard icon) at the bottom of the Composition panel. Wherever you see the checkerboard will come out transparent.
[2587] Add it to the Render Queue with <span class="k">Ctrl+M</span>, set Format to <b>QuickTime</b> in the Output Module, and set the Video Codec in Format Options to <b>Apple ProRes 4444</b>.
[2588] Change <b>Channels</b> to <b>RGB + Alpha</b>. Left at RGB, transparent areas are filled with black.
[2589] Leave Color at the default <b>Premultiplied (Matted)</b>. If you place the rendered file on a track above video in a Premiere timeline and the background shows through, you've succeeded.
[2590] If you need per-frame images, output the same way with Format <b>PNG Sequence</b> and Channels <b>RGB + Alpha</b>.
[2591] <b>When the alpha doesn't come through</b>① MP4 (H.264) and JPG can't hold transparency. ② Check whether Channels is RGB. ③ Make sure there's no solid left on at the bottom or an effect on an adjustment layer that fills the background (such as Fill). ④ If dark fringes appear around text, the recipient read the alpha as Straight; switch it to Premultiplied or re-render as Straight.
[2592] <span class="tag t1">Follow Along</span> 14-11 Image Sequences — Rendering to JPG/PNG and Importing Again
[2593] <b>A. Rendering a sequence</b>
[2594] In the Render Queue's Output Module, set Format to <b>JPEG Sequence</b> (or PNG Sequence).
[2595] For JPEG, raise Quality to <b>10</b> (maximum) in <b>Format Options</b>.
[2596] Choose a <b>new folder</b> in Output To. The file name must end with <span class="mono">[#####]</span>, as in <span class="mono">title_[#####].jpg</span>. After Effects puts frame numbers 00000, 00001… in that spot.
[2597] Render. Ten seconds at 30 fps gives 300 images in the folder.
[2598] <b>B. Importing a sequence</b>
[2599] Choose <b>File → Import → File</b> (<span class="k">Ctrl+I</span>) and select only the <b>first file</b> in the sequence folder.
[2600] Turn on the <b>JPEG Sequence</b> checkbox (PNG Sequence for PNG) at the bottom of the dialog and click Import. Hundreds of images come in as one clip. With the checkbox off, only one still comes in.
[2601] Set the sequence's speed. Select it in the Project panel and choose <b>File → Interpret Footage → Main</b> (<span class="k">Ctrl+Alt+G</span>) → under <b>Frame Rate</b>, enter 24 or 30 in <b>Assume this frame rate</b>.
[2602] For sequences with transparency, such as PNG, choose Premultiplied or Straight under <b>Alpha</b> in the same dialog, or click <b>Guess</b>.
[2603] <b>Why use sequences</b>If a three-hour render dies midway, the frames already made remain and you can resume from there. Frame outputs from Blender, C4D or AI video tools are imported the same way. Change the default frame rate in <b>Edit → Preferences → Import → Sequence Footage</b>.
[2604] 14-12 Importing a Whole Project (.aep)
[2605] To merge a scene someone else made, a logo animation from earlier work or a downloaded template into your current project, <b>import the .aep file directly</b>. No need to open it and copy and paste.
[2606] First, on the original computer, use <b>File → Dependencies → Collect Files</b> to gather the project and its sources into one folder (14-6). Move that folder into your project folder.
[2607] In your project, choose <b>File → Import → File</b> (<span class="k">Ctrl+I</span>) and pick the .aep file. You can also drag the .aep from Explorer into the Project panel.
[2608] A <b>folder</b> with the file's name appears in the Project panel, containing the compositions, sources and folder structure as they were.
[2609] If files are missing (names in italics), relink them with Replace Footage from 2-4. The other files in the same folder relink along with it.
[2610] To keep only the compositions you need, select them and choose <b>File → Dependencies → Reduce Project</b>. Unused items are cleaned out.

[2645] 16-1 How Experienced Pros Work — The Professional Pipeline
[2646] Knowing many features and doing the job well are different things. Experienced professionals use the same features <b>in a fixed order</b>. Keep to the order and revision requests won't bring the project down.
[2647] Reference and style frames
[2648] Get 3–5 reference videos and one still frame (style frame) approved first. Agree on “one picture” before anything moves
[2649] Animatic
[2650] A rough video timed with boxes and placeholder text. Lock cut lengths to music and narration
[2651] Project structure
[2652] Folders (00_Comps, 01_Footage, 02_Precomps, 03_Render, 99_Guide), naming rules (SH010_title_v003), one master composition with a precomp per shot
[2653] Rig first
[2654] Build control layers (7-16) for color, text and speed, then animate. When revisions come, change only the control values
[2655] Blocking → polishing
[2656] Linear keys for position and timing only (blocking) → speed in the Graph Editor (3-4) → overlap and follow-through (polish)
[2657] Compositing and look
[2658] An adjustment-layer stack in a fixed order: grade → glow → grain → vignette
[2659] Sound
[2660] Sound effects go in during polishing, not at the very end. Pulling keyframes one or two frames earlier to match sounds raises perceived quality a lot
[2661] Review and delivery
[2662] Safe areas, typos, disabled layers, loudness, renders per spec (14-8), backup with Collect Files
[2663] 16-2 Handling Heavy Projects — Techniques of Experienced Users
[2664] Technique
[2665] Proxies
[2666] Right-click 4K or 8K sources → <b>Create Proxy → Movie</b> to work with a light copy, and render from the originals with <b>Proxy Use: Use No Proxies</b> in Render Settings
[2667] Pre-rendering
[2668] Render a finished heavy precomp with Post-Render Action: <b>Import &amp; Replace Usage</b> and swap it for video (14-8). If changes come, switch back to the original composition
[2669] Disk cache
[2670] Point <b>Preferences → Media &amp; Disk Cache</b> to a fast SSD with 100 GB or more. On big projects the difference is noticeable
[2671] 32 bpc, linear work
[2672] Set Bit Depth to 32 bpc in <b>File → Project Settings → Color</b>. Light-adding effects such as glow, motion blur and lens effects blend naturally, like real light. Film and commercial VFX unify color management with OCIO/ACES
[2673] 3D multipass
[2674] Receive multichannel EXRs from Blender or C4D (light, reflection, shadow and depth separated) and pull out passes with <b>Effect → 3D Channel → EXtractoR</b> to grade them separately—for example, reducing only reflections without re-rendering the 3D
[2675] Several specs at once
[2676] Place the 16:9 master composition as a precomp in 9:16 and 1:1 compositions and only reposition. Changing the original updates every spec
[2677] Scripts and automation
[2678] Run .jsx files with <b>File → Scripts → Run Script File</b>, and put scripts you use often in the Scripts folder to use them as panels from the Window menu. For repetitive work such as batch renaming and arranging layers. Today the AI Assistant (Chapter 23) and the Claude bridge (Chapter 21) take over much of this role
[2679] Command-line rendering
[2680] Render without opening the window using <b>aerender</b> in the After Effects install folder. Example: <span class="mono">aerender -project "D:\job\title.aep" -comp "Final" -output "D:\job\render\title.mov"</span>. For rendering several projects in a row overnight
[2681] Version control
[2682] Keep v001, v002… with File → Increment and Save, and add _sent to the end of versions sent to clients. You must always be able to trace what you sent
[2683] <span class="tag t3">One Step Further</span> 24-4 Advanced Project — A 30-Second Cinematic Opening Title
[2684] Put the features you've learned into one scene and you get a title like a film trailer. Premium quality doesn't come from a special button but from layering <b>depth (3D, depth of field) + light (reflections, flares, glow) + atmosphere (fog, dust) + film texture (grain, motion blur) + sound</b>. This assignment follows the same order professional title designers use.
[2685] Goal
[2686] A 30-second opening in which a metallic 3D title slowly emerges from fog in a dark space, light sweeps across the letters, and it settles with a lens flare. 4K, 2.39:1 aspect ratio, 23.976 fps.
[2687] <b>1. Setting up</b>
[2688] New composition <span class="mono">MAIN_title</span>, 3840×2160, 23.976 fps, 30 seconds. Choose the <b>Advanced 3D</b> renderer. In the <b>Advanced</b> tab, set Motion Blur's Shutter Angle to 180° (the same blur as a film camera).
[2689] Set Bit Depth to <b>32 bpc</b> in <b>File → Project Settings → Color</b>. With overlapping light, colors blend smoothly without banding.
[2690] Make a 2.39:1 letterbox on top. Draw a rectangle mask on a black solid leaving the central 3840×1607 area empty (mask Inverted), and keep it a normal layer, not a Guide Layer. Name it <span class="mono">LETTERBOX</span>.
[2691] <b>2. Making the title</b>
[2692] Type the title (a bold serif or a wide sans serif, generous tracking) and turn on the 3D switch. In <b>Geometry Options</b>, set Extrusion Depth 60, Bevel Style <b>Angular</b>, Bevel Depth 4.
[2693] In Material Assignment, set Metallic 1 and Roughness 0.25. If you have a Substance metal material (.sbsar), apply brushed metal to the front and dark metal to the sides separately (23-7).
[2694] As in 13-11, set an HDRI as the Environment Light's Source and turn off the HDRI layer's eye. Surrounding light starts to reflect on the letters.
[2695] <b>3. Camera and depth</b>
[2696] Create a camera (50 mm) and parent it to a null (13-9). Push the null's Z Position forward slowly by −400 over 30 seconds (dolly in), and ease the last keyframe with <b>Easy Ease In</b> (<span class="k">Shift+F9</span>) so it stops smoothly.
[2697] Turn on the camera's <b>Depth of Field</b>, then with the text and camera selected choose <b>Layer → Camera → Link Focus Distance to Layer</b>. Focus stays locked to the text as the camera moves. Raise Aperture to increase blur in front and behind.
[2698] Make the light flow. Keyframe the Environment Light's Y Rotation to turn 60° between 8 and 14 seconds and the reflection sweeps across the letters from left to right. This moment is the title's “hero shot.”
[2699] <b>4. Atmosphere — fog and dust</b>
[2700] Using method B from 5-7, make two Fractal Noise fog layers, turn on their 3D switches, and place one in front of the text (Z −300) and one behind (Z 600). Parallax appears between the fog layers as the camera passes.
[2701] Using method ① from 5-7, make tiny dust particles (Faded Sphere, Birth Size 0.02, Velocity 0.02, Gravity 0) with Opacity 40% and Mode Add. Dust floating in the light gives a sense of space.
[2702] Give the dust layer a Fast Box Blur of 2 so it looks out of focus.
[2703] <b>5. Light — flares and glow</b>
[2704] Apply <b>Effect → Generate → Lens Flare</b> (or Optical Flares) to a black solid with Mode <b>Screen</b>. Around 14 seconds, where the reflection passes, keyframe Flare Brightness 0 → 120 → 60.
[2705] Create an <span class="mono">ADJ_glow</span> adjustment layer near the top (below the letterbox) and apply <b>Glow</b> (or Deep Glow) with Glow Threshold 80%, Radius 60 and Intensity 0.6. Only bright reflections bloom softly.
[2706] For light leaking from behind the letters, apply <b>CC Light Rays</b> to a duplicate of the text, set Center to the light source behind the letters, and use Mode Add.
[2707] <b>6. Color and film texture</b>
[2708] Apply Lumetri Color to an <span class="mono">ADJ_grade</span> adjustment layer. In Color Wheels, push Shadows slightly toward teal and Highlights toward orange (teal and orange), and lift the blacks a little with Curves for film-like raised blacks. You can also use a LUT in the Creative tab.
[2709] Apply <b>Noise &amp; Grain → Add Grain</b> (Intensity 0.3, Size 1.2) to an <span class="mono">ADJ_grain</span> adjustment layer. Digital smoothness disappears and film texture appears.
[2710] Vignette: an Inverted elliptical mask on a black solid, Feather 1200, Opacity 50%. The eye is drawn to the text in the center.
[2711] From the top, the order is letterbox → grain → vignette → grade → glow → scene. Grain always goes last (near the top).
[2712] <b>7. Sound and render</b>
[2713] Add a long low drone, a whoosh as the reflection passes, and a heavy impact at the moment the title completes. Move the flare's peak keyframe one or two frames earlier to match the impact frame.
[2714] Turn on every layer's motion blur switch and the Enable Motion Blur button at the top of the Timeline.
[2715] Render the master as 4K ProRes 422 HQ, and make the delivery version from that file in Media Encoder as 4K H.264 at 40 Mbps (14-9).
[2716] What makes it look “cheap”
[2717] Where to fix it
[2718] Everything is sharp and clean
[2719] Depth of field, grain and motion blur are missing
[2720] The light is flat
[2721] No Environment Light or reflections. Metal needs something to reflect
[2722] Motion feels mechanical
[2723] Linear keys, everything moves at once. Use easing and 0.1–0.3 s offsets (7-8)
[2724] Colors are too loud
[2725] Lower saturation and give shadows and highlights different hues
[2726] There's no sound
[2727] Half of a title's quality is sound. Re-time keyframes to the sound effects
[2728] Too many effects
[2729] Focus on one hero moment (the light sweep) and strip back the rest
[2730] <b>Working with AI</b>Fog, dust and light sources or background scenes can be generated in Higgsfield on black and laid on with Screen mode (Chapters 18–20), and repetitive adjustment-layer setup, naming and expressions can be handed to the Claude bridge (Chapter 21) or the AI Assistant (Chapter 23). People make the judgments. Deciding “where the hero moment is” is the director's job.
