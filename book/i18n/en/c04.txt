[584] <b>Arms opposite to legs.</b> When the front leg goes forward, the front arm goes back. Upper arm −20 → 0 → 22 → 0 → −20 (0, 8, 15, 23, 30f). Let the forearm follow 2 frames later and the hand 3 frames later (overlap) so the arm does not look like a stick.
[585] <b>Set the body height.</b> Change Master_Control Position Y for each pose so <b>the planted foot sits exactly on the ground line</b>. If the foot goes through the ground, reduce Y; if it floats, increase it. The Y values in the table assume Master_Control Scale 85% and a ground line at 980 px. Tilt the torso slightly forward at −4° and lift the head 3° the other way to keep the gaze forward.
[586] <b>Loop and travel.</b> Add <span class="mono">loopOut("cycle")</span> to every property with keys. To make the walk in place travel, move Master_Control Position X by twice the stride per second. The right value is the speed at which the planted foot looks still on screen.
[587] <span class="lbl">EXPRESSION · Looping and Travelling</span>// All Rotation and Position with keys — repeat 0–1 s forever<br/>loopOut("cycle");<br/>// Head Rotation — a slight nod with each step (one step = 0.5 s)<br/>3 + Math.sin(time*Math.PI*4)*1.5;<br/>// Master_Control Position — walks left while looping (match the number to the stride)<br/>loopOut("cycle") + [-time*300, 0];
[588] <b>If you are stuck</b>The feet seem to slide → the travel speed does not match the stride. Change 300 little by little to find the value where the planted foot looks still. It jumps when it loops → the values at 0f and 30f differ. The legs look tangled → the back leg is as bright as the front leg, or the layer order has changed. A gap at the knee → check that the shin is stacked above the thigh and the ball center matches the anchor point. The walk is stiff → select all keys, apply <span class="k">F9</span> (Easy Ease) and increase the overlap on the forearm and hand.
[589] <b>Changing the frame count</b>At 24 fps one step is 12 frames, with the same four poses at 0, 3, 6, 9 and 12f. A relaxed walk is 18–20 frames per step; a run is around 8 frames, with a moment when both feet are off the ground.

[698] <span class="tag t1">Follow Along</span> 5-6 Puppet Pins — Bending an Image Like a Joint
[699] Puppet pins are a tool that places pins on an image and bends the area between them like rubber. Use them for gentle motion without a skeleton: a character’s limbs, swaying grass, a curving arrow.
[700] Select the layer to bend and click the <b>Puppet Position Pin Tool</b> on the right of the toolbar (<span class="k">Ctrl+P</span>).
[701] Click pins where it should stay fixed and where it should move. The book’s sample has three pins on a vertical bar: top, middle and bottom. Turn on <b>Mesh: Show</b> in the toolbar to see the mesh.
[702] With the Selection tool (<span class="k">V</span>), drag only the top pin sideways. The other two pins hold, so the bar bends smoothly. Keyframes are set automatically on the pin positions, so dragging at different times becomes animation.
[703] <b>Figure 5-2</b>① Puppet pin tool and Mesh options (Show, Expansion 3, Density 10) ② the Puppet effect created in Effect Controls ③ three pins (yellow dots) and the mesh. Left is the original; right is the result of moving only the top pin. Composition <span class="mono">05-2_퍼펫핀</span>.
